Design · Components

Document minimap

A quiet rail of heading bars on the note's right edge that shows where you are, and expands on hover into a contents card you can jump from, modeled on Notion's floating table of contents.

TL;DR

When a note has two or more headings, a column of short bars sits on its right edge, clear of the scrollbar: one bar per heading, shorter for deeper levels, the current section's bar bright. Hovering the rail expands it into a contents card: every heading, indented and nested by level, at most two lines each, the current one in the accent color and bold. Clicking a heading moves the caret to it and scrolls it to the reading zone's top. From the keyboard, Spotlight's Show contents opens the same card with focus on the current heading. It hides only where the window has no room beside the text; Show contents still opens the card. A button on the card's first row (Dock contents) docks it flush to the window's right edge, resizable, the note's area beside it, kept on this device, in place of the rail (D-186, D-187). Component IA-CMP-MINIMAP serves UC-FIND-06, requirement UI-13, work package WP-8.8, decisions D-185, D-186 and D-187.

When it shows

The rail shows in any note, editable or read-only, on the desktop and in the browser, while all of these hold:

  • The note has two or more headings at its top level (Notion's threshold). Headings are the only structure Markdown gives a note, so the rail lists exactly what a GitHub viewer would see as sections.
  • There's room beside the text: the space between the text column's right edge and the scrollbar holds the rail (32 px), 8 px between the rail and the scrollbar, and 16 px between the rail and the text. Otherwise the rail hides rather than covering words, for example in a narrow window or beside a pinned Files panel (D-161). The card can still be opened with Show contents, over the text.

It shows in focus mode too, which is on by default: the rail sits beside the text, never on it, so it doesn't compete with the bright paragraph (CP-FOCUS-01). It also shows when the note fits the window: the rail then works as a map of the note's structure, as in Notion. The only thing stored is whether the card is pinned (below); no setting turns the rail off yet (a follow-up, if the owner wants one).

Docking

The owner asked to pin the expanded minimap. Pinning follows pinned Files (D-161): the same card, kept open, never a docked sidebar (D-186).

  • The control: an IconButton on the card's first row, at the end of the note's first heading (its text stays clear of it) (pin, then pinOff; its name and tooltip Dock contents, then Undock contents; aria-pressed). The command Dock contents (outline.togglePin, on or off) is in Spotlight, the ⋯ menu's View group and the macOS Go menu. Pressing it with the pointer leaves the caret in the note.
  • Docked, it's a column of its own (D-187, replacing the first build's floating pinned card): flush to the window's right edge from its top edge to its bottom, opaque, no shadow, a hairline on its inner side; its list starts below the title band (44 px), where the window's minimize, maximize and close stay shown, on the panel. The note's scroller, its scrollbar with it, ends where the panel starts, and the text column is centred in what's left; docking glides over motion.sheet (at once with reduced motion), the caret's line holding its height. Docked the first time, it keeps the card's width; then it's resized from its inner edge (Resize contents: drag, or ← → with Shift for bigger steps, Home, End; a double-click puts back 280 px), 200 to 480 px, never so wide that the note's area is under 400 px. Where the window can't hold docked Files, the contents and the note's 400 px, Files undocks first; narrower still, the contents go back to the rail and its hover card, the button still pressed, until there's room. Leaving it with the pointer, Esc, clicking a heading or opening another note don't close it.
  • Live: the current heading follows the caret and the scroll as the rail's bar does, and the list keeps it in view, scrolling only when it would leave the list.
  • Keyboard: the pinned card is in the tab order after the note: its pin, then the current heading (arrows move between headings). Esc in it returns the caret to the note and keeps it pinned, as pinned Files does. Show contents moves focus to its current heading.
  • Undocking: the button again, or the command. Pressed in the card, the card becomes a hover card: it closes as the pointer leaves, or on Esc or Tab if focus is in it.
  • Kept per device in state.json (outlinePinned, outlineWidth), so a docked card is there from a note's first frame on the next launch.

Anatomy

Rail
A 32 px wide column (size.hit), its right edge 8 px (space.s) left of the scrollbar's reserved gutter, vertically centered in the note's visible area and never higher than the trigger's band. It is one button: the whole column is its hit area. No fill or border at rest.
Bars
One per heading, in note order, 2 px thick (border.focus) with round ends, right-aligned 4 px inside the rail. Their length shows depth below the note's shallowest heading: 20, 16, 12 and 8 px. Bars are evenly spaced, not placed by where the heading falls in the note, so short and long sections read alike. Spacing is 16 px, shrinking so the rail stays within 70% of the visible height, down to 4 px; past that, evenly chosen headings stand for the rest and the current one is always drawn. Rest color color.textPlaceholder, quiet yet at least 3:1 on the canvas in every theme (WCAG 1.4.11: the bars are the button's only visible sign). The current section's bar is color.text and 24 px long (space.xl), longer than any level's, so it stands out by shape as well as color.
Contents card
Opens in the rail's place, its right edge on the rail's right edge, centered on the rail and kept inside the note's visible area. The popover surface (frost, 1 px color.border, shadow.2, radius.popover) with a menu's 4 px padding (space.xs), as it's a list of rows like the menus and Spotlight's results, between 240 and 320 px wide, at most 70% of the visible height and then scrolling, the current heading scrolled into view as it opens. No title: the headings are the content.
Heading items
The UI font at body size (14 px), indented 12 px (space.m) per level, 4 × 12 px padding, radius.control, 2 px apart. At most two lines, then an ellipsis; the full text is the item's accessible name, and its direction follows its text (dir="auto"). Hover and keyboard focus use color.hover, a press color.pressed; the current heading uses color.accent and semibold weight, so it isn't marked by color alone (WCAG 1.4.1). An empty heading reads Untitled heading.

Which section is current

  1. While the note has focus and the caret is in view, the current section is the caret's: the last heading at or before it. Writing in a section lights its bar. Scrolling with the wheel or the scrollbar switches to the reading rule below, even with the caret still in view, until the caret moves again (the owner, 17 October). While focus is away in Spotlight or the card, it stays the caret's, so Show contents opens on the section you were writing in.
  2. Otherwise (reading, scrolling away from the caret), it's the last heading whose top is at or above the reading zone's top line, 25% down the note (UI-11). That's where a jump puts a heading, so a heading jumped to is current at once.
  3. Scrolled to the very end of a note that scrolls, it's the last heading whose top is in view: a short last section can't reach the 25% line, and would otherwise never be current. A note that fits the window is never "at its end": the reading line decides.
  4. Above the first heading, no bar is current.

Keyboard

  • Show contents (Spotlight, and on macOS the menu bar's Go menu) opens the card with focus on the current heading, or the first. Available when the note has two or more headings; otherwise it says why.
  • In the card: ↓ and ↑ move between headings, Home and End go to the first and last, Enter or Space jumps, Esc closes it and puts the caret back where it was, and Tab leaves it (it closes).
  • The rail is a button in the tab order after the note: Enter or Space opens the card with focus inside, and Esc brings focus back to the rail. While the card covers it, the rail leaves the tab order (WCAG 2.4.11), so Shift+Tab from the first heading goes to the card's pin, then leaves the card, which closes.
  • Focus entering a card the pointer opened makes it the keyboard's: it no longer closes as the pointer leaves, and Esc or Tab close it, focus going back to the note. Whenever the card closes with focus in it, focus goes back to where it came from, never to the page.
  • A card the pointer opened leaves Esc to whatever has focus, such as Spotlight, a menu or the find bar; it takes Esc only when focus is in the note or the card.

Accessibility

  • A nav landmark named Contents holds the rail and the card, as in the WAI-ARIA disclosure navigation pattern: the rail is a button with aria-expanded (and aria-controls while open), the card a list of buttons, nested by level (a heading's subsections are a list inside its item), so screen readers hear the structure, not only the indentation (WCAG 1.3.1).
  • Items are buttons, not links: choosing one moves the caret and focus in the note, which is an action the app takes, and the editor has no anchors to link to. The current heading's item carries aria-current="location", so screen readers say where you are, not only sighted people.
  • The bars are hidden from assistive technology (aria-hidden), since the card, the command and the button's state say everything they show, but they aren't visually decorative: they're the button's only visible sign, so they meet 3:1 (WCAG 1.4.11). In forced colors, they keep system colors (GrayText at rest, CanvasText current) instead of disappearing.
  • Hover content is dismissible (Esc), hoverable (the card overlaps the rail) and persistent (no timer closes it while pointed at or focused), per WCAG 1.4.13. A hover-opened card never takes focus.
  • The card appears with a short fade and closes at once; with reduced motion, it appears without the fade.

With focus mode, find, Files, and tables

  • Focus mode (on by default) keeps the rail: it's beside the dimmed text, not on it.
  • Find sits at the top; the rail is centered below it and never overlaps the bar.
  • Docked Files is on the left the same way, the note's area between the two panels. When the window can't hold both and the note's 400 px, Files undocks first, then the contents (D-187).
  • Tables keep their own controls in the column. A table set to Bleed to edge (D-182) can pass under the rail, which stays above it and takes only its own thin column.
  • Side sheets on the right (the theme manager, note history) float over the rail.

Performance

The heading list is read from the note 150 ms after the last change, never on the keystroke (PRF-01); the caret's section is found from document positions only. Layout is measured on scroll (once a frame), on resize, and when the card opens, and nothing runs on a timer at rest (PRF-04). Opening the card or jumping is instant: no new wait for the wait inventory.

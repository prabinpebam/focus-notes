Design

Information architecture

Every view and component in Focus Notes, where it opens from, which capabilities it serves, and where its detailed design lives.

TL;DR

One home view, the canvas, with ten views reachable from it or around it and 16 reusable components. Each surface type has one job: side sheets for things you work in while the note stays visible (Files on the left, Theme on the right), a centered panel for Spotlight, dialogs for rare, focused tasks (Settings, Shortcuts, confirmations, and Use this app for yourself for visitors), and popovers for small, anchored tasks (Share, link editing). Only one panel is open at a time, and Esc always closes the top one and puts the caret back. Every entry traces to the user-can capabilities it serves. Each view is a JSON view document, and each component is delivered by a compound in the component catalog.

Surface rules

SurfaceUse it whenBehaviorUsed by
Popover, anchoredA small task tied to one thing on screenAnchored to its source, at most 360 px wide. Closes on Esc, an outside click, or finishing the task.Toolbar, ⋯ menu, Share, link editor, contextual formatting toolbar
Side sheetA task you do while seeing the noteSlides over the canvas from one edge without moving the reading column. Closes on Esc or when the task ends. Files can be pinned open: it still floats, and the reading column moves clear of it (D-161).Files (left), Theme manager (right)
Command panelTyping to find or run somethingCentered, dims the canvas, opens instantly, focus stays in the field.Spotlight
DialogA rare or focused task that deserves full attentionCentered and modal, with focus trapped inside. The primary action is on the right on every platform.Settings, Shortcuts, confirmations, conflict resolver, Move to…
ToastTelling someone what just happened, with an undoBottom center, not modal, gone after 6 s (longer while hovered or focused). Announced politely to screen readers.Undo, published, copied

Design decisions

IDDecisionServesRationaleAlternatives rejected
DD-IA-01The canvas is the only home view. Everything else opens over it and returns to the same caret.UC-CANVAS-01, UC-CANVAS-02Keeps the promise that writing is the default state (CP-CALM-01). Returning to the exact caret position removes the cost of looking something up.A dashboard or home screen listing notes. It adds a step before writing.
DD-IA-02Files is a left side sheet over the canvas.UC-FILE-03Trees need height. People expect file trees on the left (Finder, Explorer, VS Code). Overlaying instead of pushing keeps the reading column from jumping.A popover (too short for a tree), a permanent sidebar (breaks CP-CALM-01), a full-screen browser (loses context).
DD-IA-03The theme manager is a right side sheet, and the note stays visible as the live preview.UC-THEME-01, UC-THEME-08The best preview is your own note at your own size. A right sheet leaves the left-aligned start of the text visible.A modal with a sample paragraph (not your content, and it hides the result), a separate settings page.
DD-IA-04Only one panel is open at a time. Opening another replaces it.UC-CANVAS-02Stacked panels turn a calm canvas into a dashboard and complicate Esc.Allowing several sheets at once (except pinned Files, D-161: another sheet opens over the canvas beside it).
DD-IA-05Esc closes the topmost surface and puts the caret and selection back exactly as they were.UC-KEY-02A single, predictable exit makes it safe to explore any surface.Esc closing everything at once, which loses intermediate state such as a half-typed query.
DD-IA-06Settings is a dialog with categories, not the place for frequent tasks.UC-KEY-03, UC-SETUP-03Frequent tasks already have direct homes (toolbar, Theme manager, Spotlight). Settings holds the rare ones, so it can be complete without crowding.Putting theme choice only in Settings, which buries a frequent task.
DD-IA-07A missing notes folder takes the whole window, as a recovery step card; a sign-in or sync problem never does, and shows as an alert icon whose card offers the fix (D-212, D-213).UC-SAFE-08, UC-SAFE-10Without a folder there's nothing safe to show, and an empty canvas would look like lost notes. With a folder, writing must stay open, so the problem waits quietly where the person looks for sync state.Silently re-creating the folder (what happened on 24 October); a dialog over an empty canvas; blocking writing until sign-in is fixed.
